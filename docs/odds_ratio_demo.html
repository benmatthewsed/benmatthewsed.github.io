How odds ratios translate into probability differences

Each curve traces one fixed odds ratio (1–200) across every possible baseline probability. The same odds ratio produces wildly different absolute risk differences depending on how common the outcome already is. Move your mouse over the chart to trace an individual curve.

Hover anywhere on the plot — the nearest integer odds ratio (1–200) is highlighted, and its exact probability difference at that point is read out on the y-axis.
Odds2 = OR × Odds1, where Odds = p / (1−p). Comparison-group probability is solved back out as p2 = Odds2 / (1 + Odds2). Curves plot p2 − p1 across p1 ∈ [0, 1] for integer OR from 1 to 200.
